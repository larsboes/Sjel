# Source check

Use this when the user asks whether an article, study or claim holds up, or when one source
carries a decision. The output separates what the data supports from what a writer added.

## 1. Reach the primary source

Follow every citation to the study, dataset or filing it names. Read that, not the article
about it. If the primary source is unavailable, record that as a finding and lower the
confidence of every claim that depends on it.

## 2. Check three layers

Keep the layers apart in the report. The distortion usually sits in the layer that is least
open about itself.

| Layer | Check |
|---|---|
| Data | Who was sampled and how. The exact question wording. Self-report versus measured behavior. Correlation stated as cause. A missing baseline or control. Whether the full data is public. |
| Source | Who paid for and produced the work. What they sell. Which result would hurt them. |
| Coverage | Headline versus the study's own conclusion. Numbers rounded or reframed upward. One source repeated by other outlets as if they were independent. |

## 3. Report

1. **Supported:** each claim the primary data carries, with the URL and the table, figure or page.
2. **Added:** each claim the coverage or the source added, with the exact text that adds it.
3. **Unknown:** what nobody can check from the public material.

Tie every finding to a specific line in a source. "This seems biased" is not a finding.
